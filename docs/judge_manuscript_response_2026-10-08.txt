Overall verdict

This is substantially more honest than the average exploratory computational-biology project, and the manuscript does a good job retracting failed analyses rather than laundering them into a positive story. But the surviving A12 result is currently stronger as an “observation under a deliberately misspecified null” than as evidence of biological recurrence beyond chance.

The biggest statistical problem is not that p < 10^-5 is mathematically impossible to obtain. It is that the null generating mechanism is far too simple for the biological process being tested. The manuscript itself admits this in Section 3.10. A skeptical ISEF judge will notice the tension immediately:

“the same genes are hit repeatedly under the same AMP in independent lines beyond what a length-weighted null expects”

is stronger than the actual demonstrated statement:

“the observed recurrence is more extreme than expected under this particular length-weighted redraw null.”

Those are not equivalent.

I would currently classify the project as interesting and unusually methodologically self-aware, but not yet a high-level ISEF result. The strongest path is to turn A12 into a properly calibrated recurrence test rather than trying to rescue the failed prediction task or force a candidate.

1. OVERCLAIM CHECK

I am focusing on statements that either exceed the evidence or could reasonably be interpreted by a skeptical judge as a discovery claim.

A. Abstract
1. “Two descriptive observations survive.”

This is too strong because the manuscript immediately says the second organism is exploratory and post hoc.

The A13b result is explicitly not a clean replication:

“Because the mapping was chosen post hoc, this is exploratory and is not counted as a clean replication.”

So calling both things “descriptive observations [that] survive” gives A13b more evidentiary status than Section 3.11 allows.

Replace with:

“One descriptive observation survives the locked E. coli analysis; a second-organism analysis is exploratory because its gene mapping was chosen post hoc.”

That is materially more defensible.

2. “none of 100,000 length-weighted null draws reached four (p < 10−5 under a deliberately simple null).”

The numerical statement is basically defensible as a Monte Carlo tail statement, but the parenthetical can still mislead because “p < 10^-5” visually resembles a highly precise biological significance claim.

The manuscript itself says:

“the extreme tail value should not be read as a precise probability.”

Good—but the abstract gives the p-value without that qualification.

Replace with:

“zero of 100,000 draws from the specified length-weighted null reached four; this corresponds to a Monte Carlo tail estimate below 10^-5, but the null omits known biological sources of dependence and mutation-rate heterogeneity.”

Even better: reserve the p-value for the Results and put the null qualification directly beside it.

3. “Second, an exploratory and post hoc analysis shows the same phenomenon in S. maltophilia”

“Shows the same phenomenon” is too assertive.

A13b has:

14/16 events mapped only after post-hoc mapping,

T=2,

PR-39: 6/8,

LL-37/rsmH: 3/8,

shared-parent lines,

no same-gene replication of the E. coli observation,

and p<10^-5 from a null applied after the mapping choice.

This is not replication of the E. coli phenomenon in the inferential sense.

Replace with:

“A post-hoc exploratory analysis of S. maltophilia produced within-AMP recurrence under the same statistic, but the mapping was outcome-informed and therefore does not constitute an independent replication.”

4. “Independent but partial corroboration comes from Lofton et al. 2013”

This is internally inconsistent with Section 4.

Section 3.12 calls it independent functional support, but Section 4 correctly says:

“it comes from a study this lane had already mined ... rather than an independent replication of the E. coli lines.”

The experimental data are independent of Spohn. The analysis pipeline is not independent of the evidence, because Lofton was already mined during A2.

So “independent” needs qualification.

Replace with:

“External experimental evidence from Lofton et al. 2013 provides partial corroboration, although the study had already been incorporated into this project’s literature-mining workflow and is not an independent replication of the E. coli analysis.”

5. “The 09d discovery gate is unmet: no independent exact-peptide mass-spectrometry or PubMed record satisfies…”

This is mostly fine, but “no independent exact-peptide mass-spectrometry” is slightly awkward because Section 3.12 says:

“Mass spectrometry was not applicable because no peptide is named.”

That means this was not really a failed mass-spectrometry search. It was an inapplicable gate component because no candidate peptide existed.

Better:

“The 09d gate is unmet. No qualifying independent functional record was identified for the surviving genes, and no peptide-specific mass-spectrometry evidence could be assessed because no candidate peptide was named.”

B. Section 3.10 — the most important section
6. “The null redrew each line’s number of distinct genes…”

Fine descriptively.

7. “so p < 10−5 under that null.”

Technically acceptable if clearly labelled Monte Carlo tail probability under the exact simulated null, but incomplete without emphasizing that the null is biologically unrealistic.

The next paragraph does acknowledge this, which saves the section substantially.

I would write:

“No simulated replicate reached T=4 (0/100,000); the corresponding empirical Monte Carlo tail estimate is <10^-5 under this specified length-weighted null. This is not a calibrated probability for the biological null because the simulation does not model mutation hotspots, linkage, co-transcription, or within-AMP line dependence.”

That is the wording I would want an ISEF judge to see.

8. “A14 ... the verdict was unchanged”

“Verdict” is a little too grand for a descriptive null comparison.

Replace:

“A14 resolved the synonym mappings; the recurrence statistic remained T=4 with the same simulated tail result.”

That says exactly what happened without implying that A14 independently validates A12.

C. Section 3.11
9. “Then 14 of 16 events mapped, T=2 ... p <10−5.”

The number is fine as a descriptive exploratory calculation.

The danger is that readers may mentally compare p<10^-5 with A12's p<10^-5 and conclude “two extremely significant replications.”

That is not warranted.

I would explicitly state:

“Because the two additional mappings were selected after inspection of recurrence, neither T nor its tail probability has confirmatory status.”

10. “It shows the phenomenon, not the same genes.”

Still too strong.

It doesn't establish the phenomenon robustly because the mapping itself was outcome-informed and the lines share a parent.

Replace with:

“It provides an exploratory indication that within-AMP recurrence can also occur in this dataset, but the result cannot establish replication of the E. coli observation.”

D. Section 3.12
11. “Independent functional support on the Salmonella side”

For waaY, this is reasonable if “independent” means independent experimental measurement. But the sentence should not allow the reader to infer independent replication of the present result.

Better:

“This is external functional evidence from Salmonella, but it does not validate the E. coli recurrence result or rescue the failed A9 concordance rule.”

That is much harder to attack.

12. “partial corroboration”

Reasonable, but the scope needs to be precise.

Lofton provides:

recurrence of pmrB under LL-37,

recurrence of pmrB under another peptide,

functional evidence for waaY deletion,

but no demonstrated LL-37 pmrB functional effect,

and waaY is not one of the surviving A12 genes.

So it corroborates the general biological plausibility of LPS/two-component-system involvement, not A12 itself.

Use:

“Lofton provides partial external support for the broader plausibility of recurrent LPS/two-component-system involvement in peptide resistance, not direct validation of the A12 genes.”

E. Section 4 — several sentences need tightening
13. “The pivot to mutation recurrence has produced one reading that survives its own controls so far”

This is the single most problematic phrase in the Discussion.

“Survives its own controls” sounds like the finding has passed a meaningful robustness battery. It has not.

It survived:

removal of sbmA-deletion lines,

Tier B exclusion,

intergenic correction,

synonym mapping.

But the principal null itself remains simplistic.

Replace with:

“The pivot produced one reproducible descriptive pattern within the current E. coli ledger: four AMP–gene pairs recur in at least three same-AMP lines after the specified exclusions and corrections.”

Then immediately:

“This pattern is more extreme than the project’s length-weighted redraw null, but that null does not capture several known dependencies in these data.”

That is much stronger scientifically.

14. “namely that the same genes are hit repeatedly under the same AMP in independent lines beyond what a length-weighted null expects.”

This contains two problems.

“independent lines” is too casual because Section 3.10 itself says:

“Lines within an AMP are not independent.”

The biological isolates may be distinct, but statistical independence is not established.

“beyond what a length-weighted null expects” is technically true but easily read as “beyond what chance expects.”

Replace with:

“namely that several AMP–gene pairs recur across multiple lines within the same AMP, more often than generated by the specified length-weighted redraw procedure.”

That precisely states the statistical result without upgrading the null.

15. “The affected genes ... are all outer-membrane or LPS related, which is plausible but is not tested here.”

This is acceptable, but it is borderline interpretation.

It should say biologically coherent annotation, not imply mechanistic support.

Better:

“The affected genes have functions related to LPS, outer-membrane biology, or envelope-associated regulation; whether these functions explain the recurrence is not tested here.”

16. “Independent evidence that pmrB, phoP and waaY mutations arise under LL-37 passaging ... is partial corroboration of the idea that two-component and LPS-core genes are recurrent targets”

This is defensible only if the claim is explicitly about broad plausibility.

Otherwise it is a classic evidence-layer conflation:

A12:

E. coli

four particular AMP–gene pairs

recurrence statistic.

Lofton:

Salmonella

different experiment,

different genes,

functional/reconstitution results of different quality.

It cannot corroborate A12 specifically.

Replace with:

“Lofton provides external evidence that some LPS-associated and two-component-system loci can recur or affect peptide resistance in Salmonella, which is biologically consistent with—but does not independently validate—the broader interpretation of the E. coli recurrence pattern.”

F. Section 6
17. “Survives, descriptively: within-AMP Tier A recurrence beyond a length-weighted null…”

This is probably the best single sentence in the manuscript, but “beyond a length-weighted null” is still susceptible to being read as “beyond chance.”

I would use:

“Survives as a descriptive pattern: four AMP–gene pairs recur in ≥3 same-AMP lines outside the sbmA-deletion set, and this is more extreme than the specified length-weighted redraw null.”

Then:

“This does not establish recurrence beyond a biologically realistic mutation-opportunity null.”

That distinction should appear in the formal claim-state section, not merely Section 3.10.

18. “Lofton 2013 cited as independent but partial corroboration.”

Same issue discussed above. Change “independent” to external experimental evidence.

19. “The gate would close only with a targeted functional test…”

This is too narrow.

A targeted functional experiment is certainly one way to close the gate, but the manuscript's own 09d criteria require a defined perturbation plus measured phenotype. Saying it “would close only” with one experiment overstates the exclusivity.

Use:

“One route to closing the gate would be a targeted functional test…”

If the preregistration literally defines a functional test as mandatory, retain “requires,” but otherwise don't imply this is the only possible future evidence.

2. STATISTICS: A12 AND A13b
A12: what the null actually tests

The observed statistic is:

T = number of (AMP, gene) pairs with Tier A hits in ≥3 same-AMP lines.

Observed:

20 lines after exclusions.

64 mapped Tier A events in A12; 71 after A14 synonym resolution.

T = 4:

CAP18–basS: 4 lines

LL-37–lptC: 3

LL-37–wzzE: 3

HBD3–basR: 3

The null:

“redrew each line’s number of distinct genes from the 4,318 CDS in proportion to length.”

This tests a very specific proposition:

Would four or more recurring AMP–gene pairs be unusual if genes were selected independently according to CDS length, conditional on the observed per-line number of distinct genes?

That is much narrower than:

“Is same-AMP recurrence greater than expected by biological chance?”

The manuscript sometimes slides between those two propositions.

What the null fails to model

At least these:

1. Mutation-rate heterogeneity

Genes of equal length do not necessarily have equal mutation probability.

Sequence composition, replication/repair effects, local genomic context and other factors can create large differences.

The null assumes length is the relevant opportunity measure. That is not demonstrated.

2. IS-element / breakpoint hotspots

This is particularly important because Section 3.6 independently demonstrates breakpoint clustering.

You cannot simultaneously show:

“Four of nine distinct Spohn large-deletion breakpoints lie within 200 bp of IS elements”

and then treat the genome as essentially a collection of independent length-weighted CDS for recurrence inference without explaining why that mechanism cannot affect the A12 genes.

A12 excludes the sbmA deletion lines, which helps, but it does not eliminate genomic hotspot structure in the remaining Tier A events.

3. Linkage

The null treats gene selections as independent.

But the manuscript explicitly acknowledges:

“linked or co-transcribed genes”

and gives a concrete example:

“lptC and wzzE share the line LL37_3.”

This matters because the four observed pairs are not four independent pieces of evidence.

4. Operon/co-transcription structure

Same biological perturbation can produce mutations in multiple genes belonging to the same functional/genomic neighborhood.

The null has no representation of that.

5. Line dependence

This is the most important issue.

The manuscript correctly says:

“Lines within an AMP are not independent, and strata hold only four or five lines.”

The T statistic is explicitly based on multiple lines within the same AMP.

So the effective number of independent experimental units is much smaller than 20.

The null nevertheless redraws genes for each line independently.

That can produce a substantially miscalibrated tail.

6. Shared ancestry / bottleneck structure

For A12's Spohn lines, the manuscript doesn't establish that the 20 lines are independent evolutionary replicates in the statistical sense required by the null.

A shared ancestral population can create correlated trajectories.

The manuscript explicitly identifies this problem for Blanco in A13b, but the same conceptual issue deserves explicit treatment for Spohn.

7. Gene-specific ascertainment

Genes may be easier to detect/classify depending on mutation type, annotation, read quality, structural variation, etc.

The null assumes all CDS are equally observable conditional on length.

Not established.

8. AMP-specific selection opportunity

A gene may be biologically selectable under one AMP but not another.

That is actually part of the scientific question, but the null has no AMP-specific gene propensity.

The null therefore tests a much more generic random-gene model.

9. Multiple testing / researcher degrees of freedom

This is subtle.

If A12 was truly locked before looking at the relevant counts, then the nominal test is not automatically invalid simply because many other analyses existed.

But the report says in Section 5:

“Several analyses were run after the analyst had seen related counts; each lock states that exposure.”

For A12, the crucial question is not whether some analyses had exposure. It is whether:

T≥3,

the 20-line exclusion,

the selected event tier,

the particular gene mapping,

and the A12 null

were all locked before the A12 data were inspected.

The manuscript claims they were, but a judge would want the actual lock chronology/audit trail.

If those choices were genuinely locked, the A12 p-value does not need a post-hoc multiplicity penalty merely because other failed analyses existed. But if the researcher selected this statistic after seeing recurrence, the evidential status drops sharply.

Is p < 10^-5 defensible?

As a statement about the specified simulation: yes. As a biological significance claim: no.

With 0 exceedances among 100,000 simulations, you have:

0/100,000 ≥ T=4.

That supports reporting:

“0/100,000 null simulations reached T=4; empirical Monte Carlo tail probability <10^-5.”

I would not simply write:

p < 10^-5

because that looks like an ordinary calibrated inferential p-value.

And I definitely would not write:

“highly significant recurrence”

or:

“extremely unlikely by chance.”

Those imply a realistic null that you do not have.

The finite simulation also means you cannot distinguish 10^-5 from 10^-7, 10^-9, etc. The resolution of the simulation is only 10^-5.

So the honest statistical conclusion is:

The observed T=4 is outside the sampled tail of this simple null, but the magnitude of that tail probability should not be interpreted as evidence that the biological recurrence mechanism itself has probability <10^-5 under a realistic null.

That is the distinction I would insist on at judging.

A better surrogate null

Given the existing data, I would prefer a conditional permutation/null that preserves the observed dependence structure as much as possible, rather than another theoretical random-gene draw.

A good resource-bounded version:

Null A — within-AMP constrained permutation

Preserve:

AMP stratum,

number of lines per AMP,

number of Tier A gene hits per line,

total recurrence frequency of each gene,

and randomize which lines within the relevant AMP receive each gene, subject to those margins.

Then calculate T again.

This asks a much cleaner question:

Given the observed overall gene frequencies and line-level mutation burdens, do genes cluster into the same AMP across ≥3 lines more than expected?

It still doesn't solve all genomic dependence.

So I would additionally run:

Null B — genome-neighborhood/block-aware sensitivity

Collapse genes into predefined genomic neighborhoods/operons or exclude pairs within a prespecified distance and rerun T.

Critically, this must be prespecified before inspecting whether the four hits disappear.

If A12 remains unusual under both, the result becomes substantially more convincing.

A13b is statistically weaker than its p-value looks

A13b has:

“two mappings chosen after seeing which genes recurred”

That is enough to destroy confirmatory interpretation.

The p < 10^-5 is therefore not evidence that the predefined S. maltophilia hypothesis had probability <10^-5.

The mapping itself is part of the hypothesis-generation process.

The right wording is:

“Under the post-hoc mapping and exploratory null, 0/100,000 simulated datasets reached T=2.”

Not:

“S. maltophilia independently confirms the result, p<10^-5.”

The manuscript mostly understands this, but the abstract and Discussion give the result slightly too much rhetorical weight.

Correction/withdrawn-number audit

There are three places where a careless reader could still be misled.

1. A10: p = 0.0046

The report does an excellent job saying it was withdrawn.

But because the number remains in the manuscript:

“A first run ... reported W∩S = 5 with stratified p = 0.0046.”

a poster or abstract could accidentally reproduce it.

I would visually label it:

WITHDRAWN — incorrect 60-line denominator; not an evidential result.

The corrected value is:

28-line universe, W∩S=5, stratified p=0.601.

That corrected result is the one that should appear anywhere outside the deviation table.

2. A9: “8 of 120 lines”

Section 3.7 explicitly says the 120 figure is superseded by Section 3.8.

Again, good disclosure, but do not leave “8 of 120” in a figure caption, abstract, poster, or oral presentation.

It is exactly the sort of stale number a skeptical judge can seize on.

3. A12: T=6

The manuscript says:

“The first run counted yejK and yejL ... (T=6); it was corrected...”

Correct.

But because T=6 sounds substantially more dramatic than T=4, I would explicitly call it:

WITHDRAWN T=6; invalid because the run violated the locked intergenic-event exclusion.

That eliminates any ambiguity.

3. INDEPENDENCE AND CIRCULARITY
Spohn vs Lofton

The manuscript correctly distinguishes them better in Section 4 than in the Abstract.

Lofton is experimentally external, but it is not an independent replication of the present discovery workflow because the project had already mined it.

So:

independent organism/data generation: yes

independent experiment: yes

independent analysis from the project: no

independent confirmation of A12: no

external biological plausibility: yes

That is the exact classification I would use.

E. coli vs S. maltophilia

A13b is not a clean independent replication because:

mapping was chosen after observing recurrence;

only 14/16 events became mappable after that decision;

the lines share a parent;

the observed recurrence concerns different genes;

the same statistical machinery was reused.

Thus A13b is exploratory cross-organism support, not replication.

The manuscript mostly gets this right.

Lines within an AMP

This is the largest independence problem in the surviving result.

A12 calls recurrence across lines, but Section 3.10 admits:

“Lines within an AMP are not independent.”

That should be elevated from a limitation to a central inferential qualification.

You have only:

CAP18: 5 lines

PEX: 5

LL-37: 5

HBD3: 4

PR39: 1

So the T≥3 criterion is especially sensitive to what happens in the tiny strata.

In fact, PR39 contributes no possibility of T≥3 because only one non-sbmA line remains.

That means the entire T=4 signal comes from only four AMP strata.

This isn't fatal for a descriptive analysis, but it makes “independent lines” a dangerous phrase.

Shared parent

For A13b, the manuscript explicitly acknowledges:

“Blanco lines come from a shared parent, which can inflate recurrence.”

Correct.

But that means A13b should not be used as evidence that the phenomenon generalizes across independent evolutionary starting populations.

At best it shows that recurrence can arise in another experimental lineage system.

Analyst prior exposure to counts

This is not automatically fatal.

The crucial distinction is:

Legitimate preregistration

If the exact A12 statistic and null were locked before looking at the A12 recurrence counts, then previous exposure to other related analyses does not invalidate the locked A12 test.

Circularity

If the researcher:

saw the recurrence,

chose the ≥3 threshold,

selected the exclusions,

selected the length-weighted null,

then locked A12,

the p-value is not confirmatory.

The manuscript says the locks preceded execution, but the judge should inspect the actual commit timestamps and contents, not merely accept the prose.

The phrase:

“each analysis having a lock commit that precedes its execution”

is necessary but not sufficient evidence of preregistration integrity.

The relevant comparison is:

lock timestamp vs first access to the outcome data, not merely lock timestamp vs execution timestamp.

That's a real audit issue.

4. SINGLE STRONGEST UNSUPPORTED CLAIM

It is this sentence in Section 4:

“the same genes are hit repeatedly under the same AMP in independent lines beyond what a length-weighted null expects.”

More precisely, the unsupported part is the implication that this establishes non-random biological recurrence.

The data establish:

four observed AMP–gene recurrence pairs, T=4, which are rare under the specified length-weighted redraw.

They do not yet establish:

recurrence beyond a realistic mutation-opportunity process.

The manuscript itself supplies the reason: the null ignores mutation hotspots, linkage, co-transcription and line dependence.

That is the claim I would attack first as a judge.

5. ONE NEXT ANALYSIS

I would not do another literature search, another candidate scan, or another ML model.

I would do one analysis:

A locked conditional recurrence permutation
Question

After preserving the observed gene recurrence frequencies, AMP strata, and per-line mutation burdens, is same-AMP recurrence of genes greater than expected from the observed marginal structure?

Data

Only the existing 20 non-sbmA Spohn lines and their Tier A events.

No new data required.

Lock before looking at the final statistic

Predefine:

exact 20-line dataset;

exact Tier A definition;

exact gene mapping;

T = number of AMP–gene pairs occurring in ≥3 lines;

number of permutations, e.g. 100,000;

exact permutation algorithm;

whether linked/adjacent genes are excluded or clustered.

Null

Preserve:

each AMP's number of lines;

each line's number of distinct genes;

each gene's total number of line hits.

Then randomize the assignment of gene hits to lines within the appropriate exchangeability structure.

A sensitivity analysis can additionally collapse prespecified genomic neighborhoods/operons so that adjacent/co-transcribed genes cannot create artificial multiplicity.

Baseline

The baseline is the observed-margin permutation null—not a generic uniform genome.

That is substantially more credible because it conditions on much of the structure actually observed.

Endpoint

Exactly one primary endpoint:

T = number of AMP–gene pairs occurring in ≥3 lines.

No threshold shopping afterward.

Falsifier

The result is considered unsupported if:

the observed T=4 falls inside the prespecified 95% or 99% permutation distribution, or if the result disappears after the prespecified neighborhood-dependence correction.

I would make the 99th percentile the key descriptive benchmark, not hunt for another tiny p-value.

Why this is the right next move

It directly attacks the manuscript's largest unresolved criticism.

It does not demand:

more compute,

a new model,

a new organism,

wet-lab access,

a candidate peptide,

or a new threshold.

And critically, it has a credible route to a negative result.

If T=4 stops looking unusual, the project has learned something valuable: the original signal was largely explained by marginal recurrence structure.

If T=4 remains extreme, the claim becomes much more interesting.

What can be fixed now vs what requires new data
Fixable now

You can substantially improve the paper without collecting anything:

Replace “two descriptive observations survive” with one surviving descriptive observation + one exploratory observation.

Replace “independent lines” with “distinct lines” or “lines within the AMP stratum.”

Recast p<10^-5 as a Monte Carlo tail result.

Put the null's limitations immediately next to A12's headline result.

Remove “shows the same phenomenon” for A13b.

Replace “independent but partial corroboration” with “external experimental evidence, partial and not an independent replication.”

Run the conditional permutation analysis above.

Run a prespecified neighborhood/operon sensitivity analysis.

Make all withdrawn numbers visibly withdrawn.

Audit the actual lock chronology against data visibility, not just execution date.

Requires new data

A genuine biological claim requires more.

The manuscript's own Section 6 gives the right direction:

“a basS, lptC or wzzE allele measured against LL-37 or CAP18 MIC or killing in E. coli BW25113, with a prespecified direction.”

That would turn recurrence into something mechanistically testable.

But even that should be framed as one route to functional validation, not as a guarantee that the recurrence is causal.

Here I would be blunt.

As written now

Not competitive for top-tier ISEF awards.

It is intellectually respectable, but the surviving positive result is currently:

four AMP–gene pairs;

only 20 relevant lines;

four or five lines per AMP;

non-independent evolutionary lines;

a simple length-weighted null;

known biological dependence not modeled;

exploratory cross-organism support;

no functional validation;

The current manuscript would score well on honesty, documentation, and recognition of limitations, but much less strongly on demonstrated biological discovery.

You did not manufacture a positive result.

predictive failure;

enlarged-base 0.112, CI [-0.275, 0.490], p=0.281;

Society for Science

What would make it competitive

The conditional recurrence analysis is the minimum statistical upgrade I would want.

For a genuinely strong ISEF project, I would want something like:

A12 survives a biologically credible null
→ recurrence remains after dependence-aware sensitivity analyses
→ effect is not driven by one shared lineage/event
→ independent dataset with genuinely prespecified mapping reproduces the pattern
→ ideally functional perturbation confirms at least one gene/phenotype relationship.

At that point the project changes from:

“I found four things that look surprising under a simple random-gene model”

to:

“I developed a preregistered, dependence-aware test of recurrent evolutionary adaptation and identified a recurrence pattern that survives realistic opportunity controls and independent validation.”

That would be a very different caliber of project.
